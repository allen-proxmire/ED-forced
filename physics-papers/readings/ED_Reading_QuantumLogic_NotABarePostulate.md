## 1. The shrug this one refuses
Textbook quantum mechanics is a stack of postulates: states are vectors in a complex Hilbert space, observables are Hermitian operators, measurement outcomes follow the Born rule `P = |ψ|²`, and measurement collapses the state. You are told to assume all of it and check that it works. The Born rule in particular is the archetype of a bare postulate, a rule Born wrote down in 1926 with a mechanism supplied by nobody. Carroll's austere Everettian version trims the list to one item, obey the Schrödinger equation, but it is still a postulate you adopt rather than a structure you explain, and it leaves the Born rule as the hardest thing to earn back.

The Event Density claim is that most of this stack is not postulated but reconstructed, from a substrate of participating chains plus one primitive, the arrow. The honest version of that claim, told at the current tier rather than an old one, is the subject here.

## 2. What is reconstructed, element by element
Each item below was an open postulate or an assumption in the 2026-05-13 papers and is grounded or reduced in the current reconstruction. I note the shift explicitly, because a framework tightening its own joints over two months is the relevant fact.

**Why complex numbers?** Not assumed. Solèr's theorem says a Hilbert lattice of the right kind is built over one of exactly three division rings, the reals, the complex numbers, or the quaternions. ED *selects* the complex numbers from that menu: the substrate carries a scalar `U(1)` phase (P09), which the reals cannot support, and its composite systems compose commutatively, which the quaternions cannot. Tier: an account, not a from-nothing derivation, but a genuine selection from a closed list, not "assume ℂ." (May: ℂ assumed in the amplitude. Now: ℂ selected.)

**Why an orthogonal basis and an inner product?** Not postulated. Channel-orthogonality is reduced to *operational distinguishability*: two substrate channels that can be told apart perfectly are, for that reason, orthogonal (the maximum overlap of a perfectly distinguishing test is exactly `1 - c²`, which vanishes precisely when they are orthogonal). This was a bare postulate, "P-Channel-Orthogonality," in May; the keystone reduces it to distinctness-given-the-representation, and the current research-target status records it as reconstructed, with the residual now only the deep lattice-rigor step below.

**Why the Born rule?** This is the headline. The content of Gleason's theorem, why measurement gives apparatus-independent *probabilities* rather than pre-assigned values, is reconstructed from three substrate facts: each channel carries an intrinsic bandwidth `b_K` (P04), a measurement is a single commitment context, and the commitment is stochastic (P11). Non-contextuality, the load-bearing assumption of Gleason's theorem, is not assumed but *resolved*: the probability of an outcome depends only on the outcome's own bandwidth, not on the rest of the measurement. So the Born rule stops being a bare postulate. Its "why there are probabilities of this form" half is grounded; what remains statistical is the numerical value of the inner-product metric, read off as a long-run commitment frequency. The corpus's own phrase is exact-as-logic, emergent-as-statistic.

**Why a preferred basis, why definite outcomes?** Commitment (P11) is an orthogonal projection, and its irreversibility is what enforces that a repeated measurement gives the same result. Crucially, the pointer basis is not derived from the environment; **einselection is primitive**, the arrow selects the channel basis directly. This is the same claim the Many-Worlds readings reach from the other side: commitment is the fact-making diagonalization, and the basis it diagonalizes in is the one the arrow picks.

**Why is global phase invisible?** Phase is a genuine coherence observable, never a basis in which commitment happens. The arrow randomizes the phases of the un-selected channels, which is exactly what makes the overall global phase unobservable. The unobservability is arrow-tied, not a free-standing gauge accident.

**The quantum correlation bound and the value question.** The Bell-Tsirelson polytope, the boundary that separates quantum correlations from stronger-than-quantum ones, is reconstructed and stands. And the Kochen-Specker theorem is respected rather than dodged: ED gets non-contextual *probabilities* (Gleason) while denying non-contextual *values* (outcomes are committed stochastically, not read off pre-set labels).

## 3. The honest residual
The keystone states its own verdict plainly: this is a coherent reconstruction with grounded ingredients and account-tier joints, not a closed theorem. Three things are genuinely not finished, and the reading names them rather than rounding up:

1. **Solèr lattice rigor.** The technical step that carries an orthomodular lattice up to a full Hilbert space over ℝ, ℂ, or ℍ, in infinite dimensions, is the load-bearing residual. It rides underneath the whole reconstruction, including under the orthogonality reduction in section 2. This is the one deep open joint.
2. **The metric is emergent-statistical.** The *logic* of the inner product is exact; its numerical *metric* is a Born-frequency statistic, not an independently derived geometry. Exact-as-logic, emergent-as-metric.
3. **The covering law** is grounded on the channel basis, with the fuller lattice-theoretic version a candidate rather than a theorem.

So the defensible claim is bounded and, for once, quite strong: ED reconstructs quantum *logic*, the why of orthogonality, of complex amplitudes, of Born probabilities, of the preferred basis, from the substrate and the arrow, with the residual narrowed to one deep technical step and an emergent-statistical metric. That is a long way past "the Born rule is a bare postulate," and it is not "ED derives quantum mechanics."

## 4. What it says that textbook QM does not
The reconstruction is not textbook quantum mechanics wearing a new story. It makes claims the textbook does not:

- **Einselection is primitive, not decoherence-emergent.** In the standard account, no basis is special until the environment decoheres the system and picks one out. In ED the arrow selects the pointer basis at the foundation, and the apparatus-level basis-democracy is the emergent thing. This is the reconstruction-side of the Many-Worlds readings' central point.
- **Unitary evolution is punctuated, not total.** ED is unitary turning interrupted by irreversible commitment. Collapse is a substrate primitive, not an interpretive gloss added to a purely unitary world. That is the one-bit disagreement with Everett, seen from the logic side.
- **There is an accessible place to test it.** Primitive commitment predicts a quantum-to-classical boundary, a mass and complexity scale (of order `10²` kilodaltons, `Paper_060`) where superposition should stop, tracked by matter-wave interferometry as it climbs. Textbook QM has open-ended superposition and no such scale. This is the outsider's lever: the one departure that is not suppressed out of reach, unlike the ninety-orders-down footprint in gravity.

## 5. Bottom line
The Many-Worlds readings argue, interpretively, that reading the formalism with commitment as primitive pays fewer costs. This reading is the other half of the same claim: ED does not just *interpret* the quantum formalism from that primitive, it *reconstructs the quantum logic* from it, orthogonality, complex amplitudes, the Born rule, the preferred basis, the correlation bound, with the honest residual now a single deep lattice-rigor step and an emergent-statistical metric rather than a stack of bare postulates. Against "the Born rule is just a postulate," the answer in ED is no, it is grounded in intrinsic bandwidth, a single commitment context, and stochastic commitment. Against "just postulate the Schrödinger equation," the trade is visible and honest: ED postulates *less* at the quantum-logic layer and *more* at the substrate layer, the thirteen primitives and the arrow. Fewer bare quantum postulates, bought with one committed metaphysics. And unlike most metaphysics, it names the price and the one experiment, `M_crit`, that could refuse to pay it.
