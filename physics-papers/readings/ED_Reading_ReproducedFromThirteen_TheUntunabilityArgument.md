## The one-line claim
Event Density reproduces the *form and mechanism* of many separate, currently-unrelated domains of physics from thirteen substrate primitives plus a small number of labeled structural postulates. Not "it predicts what the incumbents cannot test." That room is nearly gone in a mature science. The claim is narrower and, taken seriously, harder to dismiss: **one structure gives the shape of the whole landscape, where the standard picture needs several mutually-incompatible theories and a pile of brute inputs.**

## The objection, met head-on
The reflex is immediate and fair to state: *"So what. You fit facts that were already known. Any flexible theory can be reverse-engineered to match what we already measured."*

That reflex is a **proxy**, and it's aimed at the wrong target. It treats "known versus new" as the thing that matters. It isn't. On any careful accounting of evidence, a fact confirms a theory to the degree it would be *surprising unless the theory were true* — and whether the fact was discovered before or after the theory never enters that. The timing is psychological, not logical. What the "you just fit it" reflex is *actually* groping for is a real and legitimate question:

> **Could you have tuned your theory to fit this?**

A brand-new prediction answers that airtight: the fact didn't exist when the theory was built, so it couldn't have been fudged toward. But novelty is *one* way to guarantee no-fudging, not the only one. A known fact reproduced by a structure too rigid to have been fitted to it counts exactly as much. So the real question is never old-versus-new. It is **tunable-versus-untunable.** And that is a question you can actually answer.

## The untunability argument
Two moves, and the whole case rests on them.

**1. The ratio. Few knobs, many outputs.** "I explain everything" from a theory with a thousand free parameters is worth nothing, because a thousand-knob theory fits anything. The same words from a theory with *thirteen* primitives are worth a great deal, *because you cannot tune thirteen things to independently hit gravity and the Born rule and viscoelastic relaxation and the dark sector by accident.* The figure of merit for a unified theory is not any single prediction. It is **domains-reproduced divided by free-inputs.** That ratio is the argument, and it is the exact number the standard picture does worst on, because "GR + QM + a dark-matter particle + brute constants" is four theories in a trenchcoat, each with its own inputs, two of them not even compatible.

An honest footnote, because a skeptic will reach for it: ED does also carry *inherited values* (G, a₀, ℓ_P, the masses). Those are real, and they are not zero. But they are not a fresh knob per domain — a *handful* of scales recur everywhere. ℓ_P sets Newton's constant *and* the area law *and* the Planck mass; H₀ sets the MOND scale *and* the cosmological constant. So the value layer is shared and constrained too, not tuned domain by domain. The rigidity holds at both levels.

**2. The shared route. Fit one, and the rest are forced.** The reproductions do not each get their own private derivation. They run through the *same* primitives, so fixing the structure to hit one domain constrains what it does in all the others. The cleanest specimen is the oldest one in the book, the bending of starlight:

> Fit a gravity theory to the everyday limit, falling apples and the slow bulk of Mercury's orbit, and you have thereby set how the theory warps *time*. But the same structure that warps time also warps *space*, and you do not get to choose that part separately. So the deflection of starlight is *forced* to be twice the naive Newtonian value: 1.75 arcseconds, not 0.87. You cannot fit the everyday limit and then tune the bending; one is locked to the other. ED gives the factor of two the way GR does, because in ED too a single emergent structure sets both sectors at once.

That is untunability in one number, and it is the number Eddington measured in 1919. The MOND scale is the same lesson from the other side: ED gives it as a₀ = cH₀/2π, no free coefficient, just the speed of light times the Hubble rate over 2π, and it lands within about ten percent of the measured value. There is no knob to turn. You either get that number from the structure or you don't, and it got it.

## What this is NOT
- It is **not** "ED derives the numbers." Most values are inherited. What is unified is the *form and mechanism*, not the constants. Say that first, always.
- It is **not** out-predicting GR or QM in the tested regime. There is almost no room there, and that is honest.
- It is **not** proof. Consilience *persuades*; it does not *compel*. A determined skeptic can still say "you accommodate the known facts, so what," and the only thing that closes that door is a confirmed novel prediction — for which ED has exactly one non-Planck-suppressed candidate (the sharp quantum-to-classical boundary, honestly still a shape-claim, not a number). Consilience is the body of the case; that one prediction is the intended knockout. Both, not either.
- It is **not** padded. One specimen worth naming *because* it stays out of the ledger: a single *posited* ED constitutive law has the RC circuit, the RLC circuit, and soft-matter flow as its limiting cases. That is a genuine one-equation-many-regimes result and a nice benchtop test-in-waiting, but the law is a *posited input*, not derived from the thirteen, and the circuit correspondence is a consistency check that has not been run. So it is a prediction to test, not a reproduction to count. Keeping it out of the ledger is exactly the line-drawing the whole argument depends on, and it is the discipline that separates this from the thing it would otherwise be mistaken for.

## Bottom line
The breadth from thirteen is the argument, and it is a legitimate one. Not because old facts secretly count as new — they always counted. Because **one rigid structure reproducing many independent domains cannot be a coincidence and cannot be reverse-engineered.** A theory you *could* have fitted to the data explains nothing by matching it. A theory too small to have been fitted, that matches anyway, across gravity and quantum mechanics and the bending of starlight, is telling you something about the world. That is the whole of the case, stated without a single number inflated: reproduced from thirteen, tiers in the window, the fit itself the evidence.
